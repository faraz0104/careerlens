## What DevOps Actually Means in Indian Companies in 2026

Here's the uncomfortable truth: "DevOps engineer" in India has become a diluted job title.

At a service company like **TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, or LTIMindtree**, a DevOps engineer typically does:

- Writes Jenkins pipelines for client projects
- Manages Dockerfiles and docker-compose files
- Sets up Kubernetes clusters (usually EKS or AKS) for client apps
- Writes Terraform modules for infrastructure provisioning
- Handles AWS/Azure configuration — IAM, VPC, security groups
- On-call for production issues (sometimes)
- Monitoring setup with Prometheus, Grafana, Datadog, or Dynatrace

Notice what's missing? **Software engineering.** Most DevOps engineers at Indian service companies write YAML and shell scripts. They rarely write production-grade Python or Go code. They configure tools; they don't build them.

This is why DevOps salaries in India cap out faster than you'd expect. The skill ceiling is around **18-22 LPA at product companies** and **14-16 LPA at service companies**, even with 7+ years of experience.

### The "DevOps Engineer" at Product Companies

At companies like **Razorpay, Zerodha, PhonePe, CRED, or Swiggy**, the DevOps role is usually called "Platform Engineer" or "Infrastructure Engineer" instead. These folks do write code. They build internal developer platforms, custom CI/CD tools, cost-optimization scripts, and automation frameworks. Their salary range starts at **22 LPA and goes up to 50+ LPA** with 6-8 years of experience.

So if your JD says "DevOps" but you're actually writing production Go/Python code to build platforms — congrats, you're being underpaid under a legacy job title.

## What SRE Actually Means (And Why Google Invented The Role)

Site Reliability Engineering was invented at Google around 2003 by Ben Treynor. The core idea was simple: **treat operations as a software engineering problem**.

An SRE is a software engineer who happens to work on reliability, not an ops person who happens to code. That distinction matters because it defines everything about the role — the interview process, the salary, and the day-to-day work.

In Indian companies in 2026, an SRE typically does:

- Writes production code (Go, Python, sometimes Rust) to automate reliability
- Designs and maintains **SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets**
- Does incident response and writes postmortems
- Capacity planning and performance tuning
- Chaos engineering and load testing
- Builds internal tools for observability and debugging
- On-call rotations (usually 1 week every 4-6 weeks)
- Reviews architecture for reliability bottlenecks

The key difference: **an SRE spends 40-60% of their time writing code**, not configuring tools. If you can't pass a coding interview, you can't become an SRE. Period.

### Where SRE Roles Exist in India

Not every company has real SRE roles. The companies that do:

- **FAANG India**: Google, Amazon (AWS India), Meta
- **Top Indian product companies**: Flipkart, Swiggy, Zomato, Razorpay, PhonePe, Paytm, CRED, Zerodha, Dream11, Nykaa
- **Fintech and trading**: Groww, Upstox, Angel One, Jupiter
- **SaaS**: Freshworks, Zoho (limited), Chargebee, Postman, Hasura
- **Global capability centers (GCCs)**: Walmart Labs, Target, Lowe's, Wells Fargo, JP Morgan India

If your company doesn't fit this list, the "SRE" role there might just be a rebranded DevOps role. Check the JD carefully.

## The Skill Difference: What You Actually Need to Learn

Here's the honest list of skills that separate a DevOps engineer from an SRE:

### DevOps Skillset (Table-Stakes)

- Linux administration, shell scripting (Bash)
- Docker, Kubernetes (installing, configuring, troubleshooting)
- Terraform or Pulumi for IaC
- One cloud deeply (AWS, Azure, or GCP)
- CI/CD (Jenkins, GitLab CI, GitHub Actions, ArgoCD)
- Monitoring tools (Prometheus, Grafana, Datadog)
- Basic Python for scripting

If you have 3+ years of experience, you probably know 70% of this.

### SRE Skillset (On Top of DevOps)

- **Strong DSA and system design** — because SRE interviews are coding interviews
- **One production-grade language** — Go is the most valued, Python is acceptable, Rust is a bonus
- **Distributed systems theory**: consensus, replication, CAP theorem, consistent hashing
- **Deep Linux internals**: process scheduling, memory management, networking stack, eBPF basics
- **Observability as a discipline**: structured logging, distributed tracing (OpenTelemetry), SLI/SLO design
- **Performance engineering**: profiling, flame graphs, load testing with k6/Gatling
- **Incident management**: writing blameless postmortems, running game days, chaos engineering
- **Database internals**: query optimization, replication lag, backup/restore strategies

The jump from DevOps to SRE isn't about learning more tools. It's about becoming a software engineer who deeply understands systems.

The work is deep, technical, and proactive. You're building things, not just fixing them.

## How to Switch from DevOps to SRE in India (The Real Playbook)

This is the question that actually matters. If you're reading this, you're probably stuck in a DevOps role and want to make the jump. Here's the 6-9 month plan:

### Month 1-2: Master Go (or Python at an Advanced Level)

Pick Go. Seriously. It's the lingua franca of SRE at Google, Uber, Razorpay, Zerodha, CRED, Flipkart. Build 2-3 real projects:

1. A CLI tool that parses Kubernetes pod logs and surfaces errors
2. A Prometheus exporter for a custom metric
3. A small HTTP service with graceful shutdown, structured logging, metrics

Put these on GitHub. Write READMEs explaining the design decisions.

### Month 3-4: DSA and System Design

SRE interviews at product companies and FAANG test coding. Not LeetCode Hard, but solid Medium level. You need to be able to solve 2 Medium problems in 45 minutes.

Grind **150-200 LeetCode problems** focused on arrays, strings, hashmaps, trees, graphs, and heaps. For system design, go through [system design questions](/interview-questions/system-design) and practice designing distributed systems — rate limiter, URL shortener, distributed cache, notification service.

### Month 5-6: Deep Systems Knowledge

Read these three books (or watch equivalent content):

- **Google SRE Book** (free online) — mandatory
- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** by Martin Kleppmann
- **Systems Performance** by Brendan Gregg

Learn eBPF basics. Understand how Linux schedules processes. Know the difference between blocking and non-blocking I/O at the kernel level. This is what gets you past the senior SRE interview.

### Month 7-9: Apply Smartly

Don't shotgun your resume to 200 companies. Target 20-30 companies where SRE roles exist. Get referrals — 60% of product company SRE roles are filled through referrals.

Make sure your resume is ATS-friendly and quantifies impact. [Check your ATS score on CareerLens](/resume) before you apply. Then [practice with AI mock interviews](/interview) for both coding and system design rounds.

## The Interview Process: DevOps vs SRE

The interview processes are dramatically different, and this is where most DevOps engineers get surprised.

| Round | DevOps Interview | SRE Interview |
|-------|------------------|---------------|
| Round 1 | Tool-based Q&A (Jenkins, Docker, K8s) | DSA coding (1-2 Medium problems) |
| Round 2 | Scenario-based troubleshooting | DSA + language fundamentals |
| Round 3 | Cloud architecture discussion | Systems design (reliability-focused) |
| Round 4 | Behavioral / managerial | Linux internals + networking deep dive |
| Round 5 | HR | Behavioral + "leadership principles" |

Notice the gap? DevOps interviews test whether you know the tools. SRE interviews test whether you can build the tools.

The failure mode for DevOps engineers switching to SRE is almost always Round 1 — they get rejected on coding because they underestimated how much DSA matters. Don't make that mistake.

## Which Should You Actually Choose in 2026?

Depends entirely on who you are and what you want.

**Choose DevOps if:**
- You don't enjoy coding deeply
- You want a steady 9-7 job without on-call stress
- You're OK with salary ceiling around 20-25 LPA
- You prefer configuring tools over building them
- You want faster entry into the role (2-3 months of prep)

**Choose SRE if:**
- You enjoy writing code and solving hard systems problems
- You want to earn 40-80 LPA within 5-7 years
- You're OK with 1-week-a-month on-call rotations
- You want to work at FAANG, top product companies, or GCCs
- You're willing to invest 6-9 months of focused prep

There's no shame in staying DevOps. There's no glory in burning out as an SRE at a company with a bad on-call culture. Pick based on what you actually enjoy.

## FAQ

### Is SRE harder than DevOps?

Yes, meaningfully harder. SRE requires strong programming skills (you'll write production Go or Python code daily), deep understanding of distributed systems, and the ability to clear coding interviews at FAANG-level difficulty. DevOps requires tool mastery and operational experience but not the same depth of software engineering. Most DevOps engineers can learn the tools in 1-2 years of focused work; becoming a competent SRE typically takes 3-5 years including strong CS fundamentals. That said, SRE compensation reflects this difficulty — the salary premium is 2-3x for the same experience.

### Can a DevOps engineer with 4 years of experience switch to SRE in 2026?

Absolutely, and this is one of the most common career switches happening right now. The playbook is: 6-9 months of focused prep on Go (or strong Python), 150-200 LeetCode problems, systems design, and the Google SRE book. Target companies that explicitly hire SREs — Razorpay, Flipkart, Swiggy, Zerodha, Google, Amazon, GCCs like Walmart Labs. Expect a salary jump of 60-150% in the switch. The biggest trap is applying too early before your coding is ready; most failed switches happen because candidates bomb the DSA rounds.

### Which pays more in India in 2026: SRE at a product company or SDE at a service company?

SRE at a product company pays significantly more. An SRE with 3-5 years of experience at Razorpay, Flipkart, or CRED earns 22-38 LPA. An SDE with the same experience at TCS, Infosys, or Wipro earns 8-14 LPA. The gap is 2-3x. Even compared to an SDE at a mid-tier product company, SRE roles at top product companies pay 20-30% more because reliability engineering is a specialized skill with a limited talent pool in India. FAANG SREs earn even more — 38-55 LPA at 3-5 years experience including stock.

### Do I need a CS degree to become an SRE in India?

Not strictly, but it helps a lot. Companies like Google and Amazon prefer CS graduates from tier-1 colleges for SRE roles because the role requires strong CS fundamentals — operating systems, networking, distributed systems. However, companies like Razorpay, Flipkart, CRED, and most GCCs hire SREs from non-CS backgrounds if you can demonstrate the skills. The key is building a GitHub portfolio with meaningful Go/Python projects, contributing to open source (Kubernetes, Prometheus, etc.), and clearing the technical interviews. I've seen mechanical engineers become SREs at Razorpay through sheer skill.

### Is DevOps becoming obsolete in 2026 because of AI tools?

No, but the role is evolving. AI tools like GitHub Copilot, Amazon CodeWhisperer, and specialized DevOps AI assistants are automating routine tasks — writing Dockerfiles, basic Terraform, Jenkins pipelines. This is squeezing junior DevOps roles at service companies, which is why fresher DevOps hiring is down about 25% in 2026. But senior DevOps, SRE, and Platform Engineering roles are growing because someone needs to design, review, and debug the systems that AI generates code for. The lesson: don't stay at the "YAML-writing" level. Move toward platform engineering, SRE, or deep cloud architecture roles.

## Bottom Line

- **DevOps and SRE are not the same role in India 2026** — SRE pays 2-3x more because it's fundamentally a software engineering job focused on reliability, while DevOps is a tooling and operations job.
- **If you're a DevOps engineer at a service company stuck at 10-14 LPA**, switching to SRE at a product company can realistically get you to 28-40 LPA within 6-9 months of focused prep.
- **The switch requires Go (or advanced Python), 150-200 LeetCode problems, systems design, and deep Linux/distributed systems knowledge** — not more Terraform or Kubernetes.
- **Target companies that have real SRE roles**: Google, Amazon, Flipkart, Razorpay, Swiggy, CRED, Zerodha, PhonePe, and GCCs like Walmart Labs. Avoid "SRE" roles at companies that are just rebranding DevOps.
- **Platform Engineering is the middle path** — 20-45 LPA with less coding than SRE and less on-call stress, growing fast at companies like Postman, Hasura, and Groww.
- **Don't optimize for titles, optimize for skills and compensation** — a strong Platform Engineer at a product company often earns more than a mediocre SRE at a mid-tier startup.
