Every March, Indian LinkedIn turns into a lottery result page. "Got picked!", "Not selected this year", "Third attempt, still nothing". The H1B visa continues to be the single biggest career gamble for Indian software engineers — and in 2026, the odds have gotten worse, not better.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **out of roughly 780,000 H1B registrations filed in FY2026, only about 85,000 got selected**. That's roughly an 11% chance. If you're Indian and applying through a service company, your realistic odds drop even further because of the new beneficiary-centric selection rules.

But H1B isn't dead. It's just no longer the obvious path it was in 2015. Let's break down what actually works in 2026, what the real timelines look like, and where you should be putting your energy if you genuinely want to work in the US or abroad.

## What the H1B Visa Actually Is (And What Changed in 2026)

The H1B is a **non-immigrant work visa** that lets US companies hire foreign workers in "specialty occupations" — which basically means jobs requiring at least a bachelor's degree. For Indian software engineers, this has been the golden ticket for two decades.

The visa is valid for **3 years initially, extendable to 6 years**. After that, if your employer has filed your green card, you can keep extending in 1-year or 3-year chunks. The minimum salary threshold in 2026 for a Level 1 SDE in most metros is around **$88,000-$95,000/year**, though top companies pay $130,000+ for entry-level.

### What Changed in 2024-2026

The USCIS rolled out **beneficiary-centric selection in 2024**, meaning one person = one entry in the lottery, regardless of how many companies file for them. Before this, consultancies would file 5-10 petitions for the same candidate to game the odds. That's dead now.

The registration fee also jumped from $10 to **$215 per beneficiary** in April 2024. Filing fees for the actual petition went up too — employers now pay $2,500-$5,000+ per H1B, which has made smaller companies more selective.

The 2026 lottery also saw increased scrutiny on **third-party placement** — meaning if you're a TCS/Infosys employee being placed at a client site like JPMorgan, USCIS now demands much stronger documentation.

## The 4 Realistic Paths to H1B for Indian Engineers

Not all H1B paths are created equal. Here are the actual routes people take, ranked by probability of success.

### Path 1: F1 Student Visa → OPT → H1B (Best Odds)

This is the highest-probability path. You do a **Master's in the US** (usually an MS in CS or related field), work on OPT for 12 months, then STEM OPT extension for another 24 months. That gives you **three lottery attempts** while already working in the US.

Cost: **$50,000-$120,000** total for the degree, depending on university. ROI is decent if you crack a $130K+ role at a product company.

If you're 22-26 and can afford it (or get a loan), this is still the surest bet.

### Path 2: L1 Intra-Company Transfer

If you work for a multinational (Amazon India, Google India, Microsoft IDC) for at least **1 year continuously**, you can potentially get transferred to the US on an L1 visa — no lottery required.

L1A (managers) and L1B (specialized knowledge) both work. The tricky part: getting your manager to actually push for a transfer. Most Indians who take this route spent 2-3 years proving themselves at the India office first.

### Path 3: Direct H1B Sponsorship from India

You get hired by a US company or its Indian arm, and they file H1B for you from India. This requires the company to sponsor and win the lottery for you before you can move.

Timeline: File in March, results in April, visa stamping in July-August, start in October. So from job offer to US soil is typically **10-14 months** if everything goes perfectly.

### Path 4: Service Company Route (Weakest Odds Now)

Join TCS, Infosys, Cognizant, or similar → work in India for 2-3 years → get placed on a US project → company files H1B for you. This used to be the default path pre-2020, but with beneficiary-centric rules and stricter third-party placement checks, it's become the hardest route.

If you're going this way, expect **3-5 years minimum** before you actually land in the US, if at all.

## Better Alternatives That Most Indians Ignore

Here's where I'll be blunt: **H1B is no longer the smartest move for many Indian engineers in 2026**. The math has changed. Let's look at real alternatives.

### Canada Express Entry / Global Talent Stream

Canada's **Global Talent Stream** processes tech work permits in **2 weeks**. Not 2 years — 2 weeks. Salaries are lower than the US ($90K-$140K CAD for mid-level roles), but the pathway to permanent residency is clear and doesn't depend on a lottery.

Indian software engineers with 3+ years of experience and a CS degree can score 460-490 on CRS, which puts you in the PR draw range.

### Germany EU Blue Card

Requires a job offer paying at least **€45,300/year** (about €41K for shortage occupations like IT). You get a work permit, and after **21 months of German language proficiency (B1)** or 33 months without, you get permanent residency.

Companies like SAP, N26, Zalando, and even Amazon Germany actively hire Indian engineers. English-only roles exist, especially in Berlin and Munich.

### UK Skilled Worker Visa

Post-Brexit, the UK made it easier for tech workers. You need a job offer paying at least **£38,700/year** (though many software roles pay £70K-£120K in London). No lottery, and after 5 years you can apply for ILR (Indefinite Leave to Remain).

### Remote-First US Companies

This is the sleeper play. Many US startups and mid-size companies now hire globally through **Deel, Remote.com, and Employer of Record (EOR)** services. You stay in India, get paid in USD (often ₹30-80 LPA equivalent), no visa needed.

If you want to explore this, [browse matched jobs on CareerLens](/jobs) — the remote US filter has grown significantly in the last two years.

### Australia Skilled Migration

Australia's points-based system rewards software engineers heavily. **Subclass 189 (independent)** and **Subclass 190 (state-sponsored)** are the main routes. Melbourne and Sydney have solid tech scenes, salaries range AUD $110K-$180K for mid-level roles.

## The Green Card Backlog: The Elephant in the Room

Even if you get H1B, the path to permanent residency (green card) for Indians is broken. As of 2026, the **EB-2 India backlog is around 134 years** and **EB-3 India is around 54 years**. Yes, you read that right.

This means most Indian engineers on H1B end up in what's called "**perpetual non-immigrant status**" — living in the US for 15-20 years without ever getting a green card, unable to switch jobs freely, kids "aging out" at 21 and losing dependent status.

This is why many Indians who spent 10+ years on H1B are now moving to Canada as a "**backup PR**" strategy. Canada gives PR in 12-18 months, and once you're a Canadian PR/citizen, you can cross-border commute or move to the US via TN or other visas easier.

## FAQ

**Is H1B still worth applying for as an Indian software engineer in 2026?**

Yes, but only as one of multiple parallel strategies — not your only bet. The lottery odds have improved slightly to around 24% due to fewer registrations, but the green card backlog for Indians (134 years for EB-2) makes long-term settlement nearly impossible. If you're young, single, and want US experience for 3-6 years, it's still worth it. If you want permanent settlement abroad, Canada, Germany, or Australia offer clearer paths in 2-3 years. Don't put your entire career on hold waiting for H1B — apply to it, but also apply elsewhere in parallel.

**What's the salary I should demand on H1B to make it worth leaving India?**

At minimum, aim for a base salary of **$130,000/year** at a product company, which translates to roughly ₹85-90 lakh purchasing power after taxes in a mid-cost city like Austin or Seattle. Below $110K, you're better off staying in India at a good product company earning ₹40-60 LPA — you'll actually save more money. Bay Area, NYC, or Boston require $160K+ to feel financially comfortable. Always factor in state taxes (California takes 9.3% extra), healthcare, and the fact that you can't easily go back with dignity if things don't work out.

**Can I apply for H1B on my own without a company sponsor?**

No. H1B requires a US employer to sponsor and file the petition on your behalf. You cannot self-sponsor. What you can do is get a job offer from a US company that agrees to sponsor, and they file for you in the March lottery. Some Indians take a different route — self-fund an MS in the US (F1 visa), then convert to H1B via employer sponsorship after graduation. Alternatively, the O-1 visa allows individuals with "extraordinary ability" to work in the US without a lottery, but still requires an employer or agent to file.

**What's a realistic timeline from starting my job search to landing in the US on H1B?**

Assuming you start applying today from India: **6-12 months to secure a US job offer**, then wait for the next March lottery window, then **7-10 months from lottery win to actual arrival**. Realistically, budget **18-30 months** from serious job search to landing in the US, and that's if you win the lottery on your first attempt. If not, add 12 months per re-attempt. This is why parallel-tracking Canada or Germany makes so much sense — you can be in Toronto or Berlin working in 6-9 months while still keeping your H1B application alive.

**Should I join a service company like Infosys or TCS just to get H1B?**

In 2026, this is a much weaker strategy than it was in 2015. Service companies file more petitions but win a lower percentage due to beneficiary-centric selection and stricter third-party placement scrutiny. You'll wait 2-3 years in India first, then compete internally for US project placements, then hope your petition gets picked and approved. Even if it works, your US salary will be $85K-$110K instead of the $150K+ you'd earn at a product company. Better strategy: crack a product company interview (Amazon, Microsoft, Google India), work 1-2 years, then use L1 transfer or direct H1B route.

## Bottom Line

- **H1B lottery odds are around 24% in 2026** — better than 14% two years ago, but the beneficiary-centric rule means each person truly gets only one shot per year now
- **Product company H1Bs pay 1.5-2x more** than service company H1Bs — target Amazon, Google, Microsoft over TCS/Infosys if the US is your goal
- **The green card backlog (134 years for EB-2 India) makes H1B a "US experience visa," not a settlement visa** — plan your life accordingly
- **Parallel-track Canada, Germany, or Australia** — these give PR in 1-3 years without any lottery, and salaries are 60-80% of US equivalents
- **The MS-in-US route still gives the highest overall probability** of successfully landing in the US, thanks to 3 lottery attempts on OPT/STEM OPT
- **Remote US jobs paying USD from India** are the underrated 2026 play — no visa needed, 30-80 LPA equivalent salaries, and you keep your family and lifestyle intact

The best career move isn't the one your college batchmates are all making. It's the one that matches your actual life goals. If US is a "must-have" for you, apply H1B and back it up with 2 other visa paths. If you just want a great tech career with global exposure, India in 2026 offers ₹1 Cr+ senior engineering roles that would have been unthinkable five years ago. Choose your bet with numbers, not FOMO.
