Let's cut the noise.

The headlines are confusing on purpose. One day Economic Times says "Indian IT bounces back," next day Moneycontrol says "Second wave of layoffs hits Bengaluru." Both are kind of right. The truth is messier — some companies are genuinely hiring 500+ engineers a quarter, others are doing silent PIP-driven layoffs while posting fake job openings to keep investors happy.

I've spent the last six weeks talking to recruiters at **17 companies**, scraping hiring data from LinkedIn and Naukri, and cross-checking with folks actually interviewing right now. This is the real picture of Indian tech hiring in Q4 2026 — the companies that are safe, the ones burning down quietly, and where a smart engineer should be aiming.

## What Actually Happened to Indian Tech Hiring in 2026

Quick context before the lists.

2024 and 2025 were brutal. Over **80,000 Indian tech workers** got laid off across both years. Freshers who joined TCS in 2023 waited **9-14 months** for onboarding. Startups like Dunzo, Koo, and GoMechanic imploded. Even Flipkart quietly let go of **1,100 people** in Oct 2024.

Then came 2026. The pattern shifted.

- **Service companies** (TCS, Infy, Wipro) are back to hiring freshers in bulk, but at **12-20% lower CTCs** than 2022 peaks
- **GCCs (Global Capability Centers)** — the Indian arms of Walmart, JP Morgan, Target, Lowe's — are the single biggest hiring force right now
- **Product startups** split into two camps: profitable ones (Zepto, Zoho, Postman) hiring hard, unprofitable ones (several unicorns) doing silent "performance-based exits"
- **MAANG India** is hiring, but at **40-50% lower volume** than 2021-22. Google India, Amazon India, Microsoft India are all cherry-picking.

The brutal truth: the overall tech hiring market in India is **down about 18%** from the 2022 peak, but concentrated. If you're in the right skill bucket (AI/ML, cloud, backend, SRE), you have 3x the leverage of someone doing generic Java maintenance work.

## Companies to Be Cautious About

This is the uncomfortable section. These companies are not necessarily "bad" — some are great to work at — but based on hiring freezes, layoff patterns, funding issues, or burn rate concerns, I'd think twice before joining in Q4 2026.

### Silent Layoff Patterns Observed

- **Several late-stage edtech unicorns** — Byju's situation is well-known, but a couple of other brand-name edtechs have done 10-15% quiet cuts in 2026, dressed up as "performance exits"
- **Mid-tier SaaS companies that raised at inflated 2021 valuations** — multiple have had down rounds and are cutting India engineering teams first
- **Crypto and Web3 companies** — hiring is near-dead in India. If a Web3 startup is hiring you, verify funding runway
- **Some ad-tech firms** — Google's and Apple's privacy changes hit revenues hard; India teams getting cut

### Red Flags When Evaluating an Offer

1. **The role has been open for 4+ months** — either they're being picky or they're not seriously hiring
2. **Your interviewer seems checked out or vague about team roadmap** — bad sign
3. **The offer letter mentions a "performance review" at 3 or 6 months with language about termination** — this is a PIP trap
4. **Glassdoor reviews from last 6 months mention "sudden exits" or "restructuring"**
5. **Last funding round was 18+ months ago and they're not profitable** — runway question
6. **The recruiter is hesitant about discussing team growth plans** — they likely know something

Before accepting any offer, do a basic diligence call. Reach out to 2 current employees on LinkedIn and ask: "How's team growth looking for next 2 quarters?" The answer tells you everything.

## How to Actually Get Hired in This Market

Reading hiring lists is useless if your approach is wrong. Here's what's working in Q4 2026.

### 1. Your Resume Needs to Clear ATS First

Companies are getting 400-800 applications per role. **Over 70% get filtered by ATS** before a human sees them. If your resume has fancy columns, image headers, or buried keywords, you're invisible. Run your resume through an ATS checker before applying anywhere — you can [check your ATS score on CareerLens](/resume) to see exactly what gets through and what doesn't.

### 2. Referrals Are Converting at 8-12x Normal Rates

In 2022, cold applications sometimes worked. In 2026, they don't. Referrals have gone from "nice to have" to "mandatory for mid-senior roles." Spend 30% of your job search time just building referral pipelines — comment on posts from engineers at target companies, warm up for 2 weeks, then DM asking for a referral.

### 3. Interview Prep Has Gotten Significantly Harder

Bar-raisers at Amazon India now ask **3 hard LeetCode problems** in a single round. System design is standard even for SDE-1 at good product companies. If you haven't interviewed in 2+ years, assume the bar has risen 30%. You can [practice with AI mock interviews](/interview) that simulate current company-specific patterns. For role-specific prep, dig into actual question banks like [system design questions](/interview-questions/system-design) or [React interview questions](/interview-questions/react) before applying.

### 4. Target 15-25 Applications a Week, Not 100

Spray-and-pray resumes convert at 0.3%. Targeted applications where you spend 30 minutes customizing resume + writing a tailored message convert at 8-12%. The math is obvious.

### 5. Negotiate Even in a "Weak" Market

Companies are using layoff news to lowball offers. Push back. The market for *good* engineers is still competitive. A 20-25% hike should be your floor, 40-50% is achievable with 2 competing offers.

## FAQ

**Q: Are Indian IT layoffs still happening in late 2026 or has it stabilized?**

Mostly stabilized, but with specific pockets of pain. Mass layoffs at Infosys, TCS, Wipro levels are over — those companies are back to net-positive hiring. However, mid-tier SaaS unicorns and late-stage startups with runway issues are still doing quiet, performance-based cuts. The pattern now is **targeted 5-10% trims** at individual companies rather than industry-wide layoff waves. If you're at a well-funded or profitable company, your job is reasonably safe. If you're at a Series C-D unicorn with no clear path to profitability and the last funding was 18+ months ago, start interviewing elsewhere now.

**Q: Which Indian tech companies have NOT done any layoffs since 2023?**

Zoho has famously never done layoffs. JP Morgan India, Walmart Global Tech, Target India, and most banking GCCs have been consistent hirers through the entire downturn. Among product companies, Zepto, Postman, Groww, and Razorpay have avoided mass layoffs (though Razorpay did some restructuring). Google India and Microsoft India had small cuts in 2023-24 but have been net-positive hirers since. Zoho and the top GCCs are the safest bets if job security is your highest priority — you trade some upside pay for genuine stability.

**Q: Should I accept an offer from a startup in 2026 given the layoff risk?**

Depends entirely on the specific startup. Rule of thumb: check their last funding round date (if 24+ months old and not profitable, avoid), check Glassdoor for recent reviews mentioning "layoffs" or "restructuring," and ask the hiring manager directly: "How is team growth looking for the next 4 quarters?" Profitable startups like Zepto, Zoho, Postman, Groww, and Razorpay are safe. Unprofitable Series C/D companies in competitive spaces (consumer, edtech, Web3) carry real risk. ESOPs from risky startups are mostly worthless — negotiate higher cash CTC instead.

**Q: Are freshers getting hired in Indian IT in 2026?**

Yes, significantly more than in 2024. TCS, Infosys, Wipro, and HCL are back to volume hiring freshers at **3.4-4.5 LPA**. Cognizant, LTIMindtree, Capgemini, and Accenture are also back on campus. However, product companies and GCCs still prefer 1-2+ years experience — fresher roles there are limited and highly competitive. If you're a fresher, your realistic first job is a service company (which is fine as a launchpad), and your plan should be to switch to a product company or GCC after 18-24 months.

**Q: Is now a good time to switch jobs or should I wait for 2027?**

Now is actually better than waiting. Hiring budgets at GCCs and product companies are opening up for Q1 2027 joiners, which means interviewing in Oct-Dec gives you the best shot. Waiting till March-April means competing with everyone who got a bad appraisal. Average salary hikes on switch in Q4 2026 are **35-55%** for good candidates, versus 8-12% internal appraisals. If you've been at your current company 2+ years and haven't gotten promoted or hiked significantly, the opportunity cost of waiting is real money.
