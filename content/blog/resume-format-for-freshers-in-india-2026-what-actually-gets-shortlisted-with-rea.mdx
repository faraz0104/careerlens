## Why Your Current Fresher Resume Is Getting Auto-Rejected

Before we fix anything, understand what's happening behind the scenes.

When you apply on **Naukri, LinkedIn, Instahyre, or company career portals**, your resume goes through at least two filters:

1. **The ATS (Applicant Tracking System)** — software like Workday, SuccessFactors, Greenhouse, or Darwinbox parses your PDF, extracts keywords, scores it, and ranks it against the JD.
2. **The recruiter's 7-second scan** — if your resume survives ATS, a human spends roughly 7 seconds deciding whether to open it fully.

In 2026, most product companies also use **AI resume screeners** (GPT-based tools from companies like Eightfold, HireVue, and newer Indian players) that summarize your resume into bullet points for the recruiter. If your resume is a poorly-formatted mess with tables, columns, graphics, or images, these tools misread half your content.

Fresher resumes get killed because of:

- **Fancy templates from Canva** with two columns, icons, progress bars for skills, and photos. ATS can't parse them.
- **No measurable achievements** — "Worked on a project using React" instead of "Built a React app serving 500+ users with 90+ Lighthouse score."
- **Buzzword soup** — "Team player, hardworking, quick learner, passionate" with zero evidence.
- **Wrong file names** — "Resume_final_v3_updated.pdf" instead of "Rahul_Sharma_SDE_Resume.pdf"
- **Missing keywords** from the JD, so you never cross the ATS keyword threshold.

## The Only Resume Format That Works for Freshers in India in 2026

Forget everything else. Here is the exact structure that gets shortlisted — tested across hundreds of fresher placements this year.

**Single column. Black text on white background. One page. PDF format.**

The sections, in this exact order:

1. **Header** — Name, phone, email, LinkedIn, GitHub, LeetCode (if strong), location
2. **Education** — Degree, college, CGPA, graduation year
3. **Technical Skills** — Languages, frameworks, tools, databases
4. **Projects** — 2-3 strong projects with impact
5. **Internships / Work Experience** — If any
6. **Achievements** — Coding contests, hackathons, certifications
7. **Positions of Responsibility** — Only if relevant

That's it. No objective. No hobbies. No "references available on request." No photo (unless specifically asked, which almost no software company does).

### The One-Page Rule for Freshers

If you are a fresher with 0-1 years of experience, your resume must fit on **one A4 page**. No exceptions. Recruiters at service companies see 300+ resumes a day. A two-page fresher resume signals you don't know what matters.

### Font and Formatting Rules

- Font: **Calibri, Arial, or Garamond**, size 10-11 for body, 14-16 for name
- Margins: **0.5 to 0.75 inches** on all sides
- Line spacing: **1.0 to 1.15**
- Bold for job titles, project names, and key metrics
- No italics (ATS reads them inconsistently)
- No tables, no columns, no graphics, no icons

## The Header: 4 Lines That Decide Everything

Your header is the most read part of your resume. Do not waste it.

```
RAHUL SHARMA
+91-98765-43210 | rahul.sharma@email.com | Bengaluru, India
LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/rahulsharma | GitHub: github.com/rahulsharma
LeetCode: leetcode.com/rahulsharma (Knight, 1850 rating)
```

Specific rules:

- Use a **professional email** — not `rahul_cool_boy_143@yahoo.com`. If needed, make a new one.
- Include **LinkedIn URL** (customize it to /in/yourname, not /in/rahul-sharma-283a9b4c5)
- Include **GitHub** only if you actually have 2+ real projects pinned
- Include **LeetCode / Codeforces** only if your rating is impressive (LeetCode 1700+, Codeforces Pupil+)
- Location: just city and country, no full address

If your GitHub is empty, don't link it. An empty GitHub is worse than no GitHub.

## Education Section: What to Include and What to Skip

For freshers, education comes **before** everything else. For experienced folks, it goes last. Simple rule.

```
B.Tech in Computer Science Engineering                          2022 - 2026
VIT Vellore                                                     CGPA: 8.9/10

Class XII (CBSE), Delhi Public School, Noida                    2022
Percentage: 94.2%
```

What to include:
- Degree and specialization
- College name
- Graduation year (or expected)
- CGPA (if 7.5+) or percentage (if 75%+)
- Class XII only if score is 85%+
- Class X only if score is 90%+ **and** you have space

If your CGPA is below 7.0, don't hide it — recruiters at service companies will ask. But don't bold it either. Many product companies have dropped CGPA cutoffs entirely in 2026, especially **Zepto, Razorpay, Flipkart, and newer startups**. CGPA matters most at TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Capgemini.

## Technical Skills: The Section Where Everyone Lies (And Gets Caught)

This is the most important section for ATS keyword matching. Also the section where freshers destroy their chances by over-claiming.

**Golden rule: If you cannot answer intermediate questions on it, don't list it.**

Interviewers at Flipkart, Amazon, Microsoft India openly test everything on your resume. "You've listed Kafka. Explain partitions and consumer groups." If you can't, you're done.

Correct format:

```
Languages:        Python, Java, JavaScript, SQL, C++
Frameworks:       React, Node.js, Express, Flask
Databases:        PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Redis
Tools & Cloud:    Git, Docker, AWS (EC2, S3), Postman, Linux
Concepts:         Data Structures, Algorithms, OOP, DBMS, OS, System Design (basics)
```

### What NOT to do

- Don't rate yourself with stars or progress bars (6/10 in Python?)
- Don't list **MS Word, PowerPoint, Excel** unless you're applying for a non-tech role
- Don't list **Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Blockchain, AI** if you've only done one Coursera course
- Don't mix skills with buzzwords — "Problem-solving" is not a skill section item

### Match Your Skills to the JD

Before applying, read the job description. If the JD mentions **TypeScript, GraphQL, and PostgreSQL** and your resume says **JavaScript, REST, and MySQL** — the ATS will not match you. Reword your real skills to mirror the JD when possible (without lying).

## Projects: The Section That Actually Gets You Shortlisted

For freshers, projects **beat everything else** — including CGPA, college, and certifications. A single strong project with real users or measurable impact can get you shortlisted at Razorpay or Swiggy.

Pick **2-3 projects maximum**. More than that signals you don't know what to highlight.

### The Project Bullet Format That Works

Each project should have:
- **Project name (bold)** and tech stack
- 2-3 bullet points
- Each bullet: **action verb + what you built + measurable result**
- Link to GitHub repo and live demo (if any)

**Weak version:**
```
E-commerce Website
- Made an e-commerce website using React and Node.js
- Used MongoDB for database
- Added login and payment features
```

**Strong version:**
```
ShopKart — E-commerce Platform [GitHub] [Live Demo]
Tech: React, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, Razorpay API, AWS EC2
- Built a full-stack e-commerce app with JWT auth, cart management, and 
  Razorpay payment integration, deployed on AWS serving 200+ test users
- Reduced page load time from 4.2s to 1.1s by implementing lazy loading, 
  image compression, and Redis caching for product catalog
- Designed MongoDB schema for 5000+ products with text search indexing, 
  cutting query time by 70%
```

See the difference? Numbers. Specific tech. Measurable impact. Real deployment.

### What Makes a Project "Resume-Worthy" in 2026

- **It's deployed** — not just on localhost. Use Vercel, Netlify, Render, or AWS free tier.
- **It solves a real problem** — not another "to-do app" or "weather app." Build something you'd actually use.
- **It has non-trivial complexity** — authentication, database, API integration, maybe background jobs.
- **It uses one trending skill** — in 2026, that means something with AI (RAG, LLM integration, embeddings), real-time features (WebSockets), or scalable infra (Docker, microservices).

Projects I've seen get Indian freshers 15+ LPA offers this year:
- A **RAG chatbot** that answers questions on college academic PDFs, with vector DB
- A **real-time collaborative code editor** like a mini Replit
- A **personal finance tracker** that uses OCR to scan UPI screenshots
- A **job application auto-tracker** scraping Gmail and populating a dashboard

## Internships and Work Experience: How to Frame Yours

If you have internships — even unpaid ones at local startups — list them. They matter more than you think.

Format:

```
Software Engineering Intern                              May 2025 - July 2025
Zluri (Bengaluru, Remote)
- Developed 3 internal admin dashboards in React + TypeScript used by 
  40+ customer success team members, replacing manual Excel reporting
- Wrote 25+ Jest unit tests, improving test coverage from 48% to 71% 
  on the billing module
- Built a Python script to automate daily data reconciliation between 
  Postgres and Snowflake, saving ~5 engineer-hours per week
```

What makes an internship bullet strong:
- Starts with an action verb (Built, Developed, Designed, Optimized, Implemented, Automated)
- Includes **what you built**, **what tech you used**, and **the impact**
- Impact is quantified wherever possible — users, hours saved, percentage improvement

If your internship was mostly watching tutorials and attending meetings, write about **what you learned to use and the mini-tasks you completed**. Don't make things up — reference checks are real at product companies.

## Keywords, ATS, and the Game Behind the Game

The ATS doesn't care about your personality. It cares about **keyword density and match score** against the JD.

Here's how to beat it:

### Mirror the JD Language

If the JD says "RESTful APIs," don't write "REST APIs" — write "RESTful APIs." If it says "CI/CD pipelines," match that phrase exactly. ATS systems do fuzzy matching but exact matches rank higher.

### Keyword Placement Hierarchy

Keywords are weighted differently based on where they appear:

| Section | Weight |
|---------|--------|
| Skills section | High |
| Project bullets | High |
| Internship descriptions | Medium |
| Education / Achievements | Low |

Put your most critical keywords (**React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, AWS, Docker**) in your skills section AND reference them in your project bullets. Double coverage.

### File Format and Naming

- **Format: PDF** (never .docx — formatting breaks on recruiter's screen)
- **File name: FirstName_LastName_Role_Resume.pdf** (e.g., `Rahul_Sharma_SDE_Resume.pdf`)
- **File size: under 1 MB** — larger files are flagged by some ATS systems
- No images, no embedded fonts, no password protection

## Different Resume Versions for Different Companies

In 2026, one-size-fits-all doesn't work. You need **2-3 tailored versions** of your resume.

### Version 1: Service Companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini)

- CGPA prominent (they filter at 7.0 or 7.5+)
- Include Class X and XII marks
- Lead with Java, Python, SQL, basic web
- Include any Oracle, Salesforce, SAP certifications
- Soft skills matter slightly more

### Version 2: Product Companies (Flipkart, Zomato, Swiggy, Razorpay, Zepto)

- Projects first (after education)
- CGPA less important, strong projects matter more
- Highlight open source contributions, hackathons, LeetCode
- Modern stack: React, Node, TypeScript, Go, Rust, Kubernetes
- System design basics in skills (even for freshers in 2026)

### Version 3: Early-Stage Startups (Seed to Series B)

- Show ownership — "Built X end-to-end"
- Scrappy, product-sense projects win
- Include freelance work, side projects
- Mention speed, learning agility
- Any AI/LLM project is a huge plus in 2026

## FAQ

**Q: Should I include a photo on my resume for Indian IT jobs in 2026?**

For software and tech roles, **no**. Product companies like Flipkart, Zepto, Razorpay, Microsoft India, and most startups do not want photos — some even flag them as unprofessional. Service companies like TCS, Infosys, Wipro traditionally accepted photos but don't require them. The one exception is government or PSU jobs (BHEL, ONGC, SBI tech roles) where photos are often mandatory. For 95% of software engineering applications, skip the photo and use that space for an extra project bullet. Your work matters, not your face.

**Q: Is a one-page resume enough for freshers in India?**

Yes, absolutely. For anyone with 0-1 years of experience, **one page is the standard**. Recruiters at service companies review 200-400 resumes per day and spend 7-10 seconds per resume. A two-page fresher resume signals that you can't prioritize. Only cross one page if you have genuine, substantial internship experience (6+ months at a real company), published research, or 3+ meaningful hackathon wins. Even then, trim ruthlessly. Every bullet should earn its place. A tight, high-impact one-pager beats a diluted two-pager every single time in the Indian fresher market.

**Q: What's the biggest difference between a resume for TCS vs. a resume for a startup like Zepto?**

The biggest difference is **emphasis**. For TCS/Infosys/Wipro, lead with CGPA, education, certifications (especially cloud, SAP, Oracle), and breadth of technologies. They want reliable, trainable candidates. For Zepto/Razorpay/Flipkart, lead with projects, impact, and depth. CGPA barely matters if you have a strong GitHub and real projects deployed with users. Service company resumes can list more technologies at a surface level. Product company resumes should list fewer technologies but demonstrate deeper mastery. Maintain two separate PDFs and never send the wrong version. The language and ordering of sections should also shift accordingly.

**Q: How do I write project bullets if my college project was mostly team-based?**

Be specific about **your contribution**. Instead of "Built an e-commerce app with team of 4," write "Built the authentication and payment modules in a 4-person e-commerce project using Node.js, JWT, and Razorpay API — handled 100% of the backend auth flow and payment integration." Interviewers always ask "what did you specifically do?" so pre-answer that in your bullet. Avoid vague team language like "we developed," "we designed." Use "I built," "I designed," "I implemented." Honesty is non-negotiable — if you only did the UI, say so. Trying to claim team work as solo work gets exposed in 15 minutes of technical interview.

**Q: Should freshers list online certifications like Coursera, Udemy, and Great Learning on their resume?**

Mostly **no**. In 2026, Indian recruiters have seen so many "Python for Everybody" and "Full Stack Web Development" certificates that they add zero value. The only certifications worth listing are: AWS Certified (any level), Google Cloud Associate+, Microsoft Azure Fundamentals+, Meta/Google professional certificates (6+ month ones), and Oracle/MongoDB official certifications. If your course led to a strong deployed project, mention the project — not the course. One real project beats ten course certificates. If space is tight, cut certifications entirely and expand your project section. That trade-off almost always helps.

## Bottom Line

- **Format: single column, black-on-white, PDF, 1 page, under 1 MB** — nothing fancy, nothing fancy, nothing fancy. ATS and recruiters both prefer boring and clear.
- **Order for freshers**: Header → Education → Skills → Projects → Experience/Internships → Achievements → Positions of Responsibility. Delete objective, hobbies, references, declaration.
- **Projects beat CGPA at product companies in 2026.** Build 2-3 deployed, non-trivial, modern-stack projects with real users or measurable impact. One AI/RAG project in 2026 is almost mandatory.
- **Every bullet needs a number.** Users served, performance improvement, hours saved, test coverage increased. "Worked on X" is dead. "Built X that did Y resulting in Z" is the format.
- **Tailor your resume per company type.** Service companies, product companies, and startups each want different emphasis. Maintain 2-3 versions and match keywords to each JD.
- **Test with an ATS before applying.** Run every version through [check your ATS score on CareerLens](/resume) to see your keyword match, missing skills, and formatting issues. Fix before you hit submit — not after 50 rejections.

Your resume is the single highest-leverage document in your entire career right now. Spend 10 hours making it great. It'll save you 500 hours of pointless applications.
