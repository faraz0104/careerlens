Let's be honest. Infosys hiring in 2026 is nothing like what your seniors from 2022 batch described.

The company hired around **16,000 freshers in FY26** so far, down from the 50,000+ sprees of the pandemic years. The bar is higher. The process is tighter. And the "automatic selection if you clear aptitude" era is dead.

But here's the good news: Infosys still runs one of the most **structured, predictable interview processes** in the Indian IT industry. If you know exactly what each round tests, you can walk in prepared instead of panicking.

This is the complete breakdown — every round, real questions from October 2026 drives, salary bands, and what the interviewer is actually looking for behind each question.

## The Three Entry Routes Into Infosys in 2026

Before we talk rounds, understand this: there is no single "Infosys interview." There are three entirely different hiring tracks, and the process depends on which one you're in.

| Route | Role | CTC (2026) | Who it's for |
|-------|------|------------|--------------|
| **System Engineer (SE)** | Generalist developer | ₹3.6 LPA | Mass hiring, campus + off-campus |
| **Digital Specialist Engineer (DSE)** | Specialist in cloud/AI/full-stack | ₹6.5 LPA | Strong coders, Hackwithinfy finalists |
| **Power Programmer (PP)** | Elite tech track | ₹9–11 LPA | Top coders, 5+ star HackerRank |

The System Engineer role is what 80% of candidates get. DSE is where you want to aim if you've been coding seriously. Power Programmer is the dream — fewer than 500 offers a year across India.

The process below covers the **InfyTQ-based hiring** (which is now Infosys's primary off-campus channel) and the on-campus process. They're 90% identical in 2026.

### Why Infosys Changed Its Process in 2026

Two reasons. First, the bench was costing them money — too many SEs who couldn't code past "Hello World" sat idle for 6+ months. Second, GenAI pressure. Infosys Topaz needs engineers who can actually build, not just maintain.

So the aptitude got easier (to filter faster), but the coding and technical rounds got meaningfully harder. You cannot bluff your way through the technical interview anymore. I've seen students with 9+ CGPA get rejected because they couldn't explain their own project.

## Round 1: Online Assessment (The InfyTQ / HackWithInfy Test)

This is your filter round. Everyone takes it. You get **195 minutes** total, split across sections.

**Section-wise breakdown (2026 pattern):**

- **Reasoning Ability** — 15 questions, 25 minutes. Puzzles, data arrangements, syllogisms.
- **Mathematical Ability** — 10 questions, 35 minutes. Permutations, probability, time-speed-distance.
- **Verbal Ability** — 20 questions, 20 minutes. Reading comprehension, sentence correction.
- **Pseudocode** — 5 questions, 10 minutes. Output of pseudocode snippets.
- **Puzzle Solving** — 4 questions, 35 minutes. Logical arrangement.
- **Coding Section** — 2 problems, 60 minutes.

The coding section is where most candidates get eliminated now. In 2024, you could clear with one solved problem. In **2026, you need both problems at least partially passing** to move forward with any confidence.

### Real Coding Problems From October 2026 Drives

Here's what candidates actually got in recent drives:

1. **Given a string, find the longest substring with at most K distinct characters.** (Medium — sliding window.)
2. **An array of meeting intervals. Find the minimum number of conference rooms needed.** (Medium — heap/sorting.)
3. **Given a 2D grid of 0s and 1s, find the shortest path from top-left to bottom-right where you can flip at most one 0 to 1.** (Hard-ish — BFS with state.)

If these problems make you nervous, you need serious DSA practice before applying. [Browse Infosys interview questions on CareerLens](/interview-questions/dsa) to see the full question bank with solutions and difficulty tags.

### Cutoff Benchmarks

- **System Engineer cutoff:** ~65% overall, both coding problems with at least 1 fully solved
- **Digital Specialist cutoff:** ~75% overall, both coding problems fully solved
- **Power Programmer cutoff:** Top 1% — essentially all problems solved perfectly

## Round 2: Technical Interview (The Make-or-Break Round)

This is where 60% of post-aptitude candidates get rejected. Average duration: **35-50 minutes**.

The interviewer is usually a 3-7 year experienced engineer, often from Infosys Pune, Bangalore, or Hyderabad DCs. They have 10 interviews that day. They are tired. They want to see if you can actually code and think.

### What They Actually Ask (October 2026 Pattern)

The interview typically has four phases:

**Phase 1: Introduction (3-5 min)**
"Tell me about yourself" — but focus on your tech stack, not your hobbies. Nobody cares you like reading novels.

**Phase 2: Project Deep-Dive (15-20 min)**
This is where candidates die. Pick ONE project from your resume. Know it cold.

Expected questions:
- Why did you choose this tech stack over alternatives?
- Walk me through what happens when a user clicks login
- What's the database schema? Why not NoSQL?
- How would you scale this to 10,000 users?
- What was the hardest bug you fixed?

If your project is a tutorial clone of "Netflix frontend," you will struggle. Build something you can actually defend. A functional **inventory system for your college canteen** beats a half-built Netflix clone every single time.

**Phase 3: Technical Fundamentals (10-15 min)**
Expect rapid-fire from your core subjects:

- **DBMS:** Normalization (1NF to 3NF), ACID properties, joins, indexes
- **OOP:** Four pillars, abstract class vs interface, method overloading vs overriding
- **OS:** Process vs thread, deadlock conditions, paging vs segmentation
- **DSA:** Time complexity of common operations, when to use HashMap vs TreeMap

**Phase 4: Live Coding (10-15 min)**
Usually a medium LeetCode problem on a shared editor (CodePair or similar).

Real questions from 2026:
- Reverse a linked list (iterative AND recursive)
- Find the second largest element without sorting
- Check if a string has balanced parentheses
- Find all pairs in an array summing to target K

### What Makes You Stand Out

I've debriefed dozens of successful Infosys candidates. The pattern is consistent:

1. **Think out loud.** Silence is suspicious. Narrate your approach even if wrong.
2. **Ask clarifying questions.** "Should I handle empty input?" shows maturity.
3. **Discuss time/space complexity BEFORE coding.** Don't wait to be asked.
4. **Admit when you don't know.** "I haven't worked with Kafka, but based on what I know about message queues..." is 10x better than making stuff up.

## Round 3: Managerial Round (Not Every Candidate Gets This)

Only candidates targeted for DSE or Power Programmer roles consistently see this round. For System Engineer, this is often merged into HR.

Duration: 20-30 minutes. The interviewer is usually a Project Manager or Delivery Lead.

### The Questions You'll Face

- "Tell me about a time you worked in a team conflict."
- "A client wants a feature in 3 days. Your tech lead says it needs 2 weeks. What do you do?"
- "Have you ever failed at something? What did you learn?"
- "Why Infosys and not TCS or Wipro?"
- "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"

The **STAR method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is non-negotiable here. Vague answers like "I'm a team player" will get you rejected. Specific stories with outcomes get you hired.

### A Real STAR Answer That Worked

Question: "Tell me about a leadership experience."

Weak answer: "I was the team lead for my college project and I motivated my team."

Strong answer: "In my final semester project, 2 of 4 teammates stopped contributing by week 3. Instead of complaining to the professor, I restructured the work — I took their frontend modules, gave the remaining active member the backend integration, and we did daily 15-minute standups. We submitted 3 days early and scored the highest in our batch. I learned that leading isn't about motivating — it's about redistributing when reality doesn't match your plan."

That's the difference.

## Round 4: HR Interview (The One Everyone Underestimates)

Here's the truth nobody tells you: **the HR round at Infosys has a real rejection rate**. It's not a formality.

In October 2026 drives, roughly 15-20% of candidates who clear technical get rejected in HR. Reasons:
- Negative attitude about relocation
- Unrealistic salary expectations
- Bad body language on video calls
- Red flags about commitment (asking too early about leaves, WFH, bond)

Duration: 15-25 minutes.

### Questions That Actually Get Asked

- Tell me about yourself (yes, again — different interviewer)
- Why Infosys?
- Are you comfortable relocating to Mysore for training?
- Are you okay with the 2-year service agreement?
- What's your expected CTC?
- Do you have any backlogs?
- Any gaps in your education?

### The Mysore Training Question — Handle With Care

Infosys Mysore training is **4-5 months long**. You WILL be sent there if you join. If you show any hesitation, you're done.

Right answer: "Yes, absolutely. I'm actually excited — I've heard the Mysore campus is one of the best corporate training facilities globally."

Wrong answer: "Umm, my parents might have an issue because my grandfather is unwell..."

Save personal constraints for after the offer letter.

### The Service Agreement Question

Infosys has a **₹1 lakh bond for 2 years** (updated in 2025). If you leave before 2 years, you pay this amount. It's legal and enforceable in most cases. If you're unsure how bonds work, understand [the legality of bonds in Indian IT companies](/blog/bond-and-service-agreement-in-indian-it-companies-2026-are-they-legal-enforceabl) before signing anything.

In the interview, don't argue. Just say: "I'm aware of the service agreement and I'm comfortable with it. I'm looking at this as a long-term career move."

## Lateral Hire Interview Process (3+ Years Experience)

If you're a lateral candidate with 3-10 years of experience applying for roles like Senior Systems Engineer, Technology Analyst, or Technology Lead — the process is different.

**Typical lateral interview flow:**

| Round | Duration | What they test |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **Recruiter Screening** | 15 min | Fit, CTC, notice period |
| **Technical Round 1** | 60 min | Deep tech + system design |
| **Technical Round 2** | 60 min | Code pairing + architecture |
| **Managerial Round** | 45 min | Project leadership, client handling |
| **HR Discussion** | 30 min | Offer, negotiation |

Lateral candidates face harder system design questions. Expect:
- Design a URL shortener
- Design a notification system handling 1M events/day
- How would you migrate a monolith to microservices?

## The 60-Day Prep Plan That Actually Works

If you have 2 months before your Infosys interview, here's what to do:

**Days 1-20: Foundations**
- Finish 150 easy + medium LeetCode problems (focus on arrays, strings, hashmaps)
- Revise DBMS: normalization, SQL joins, indexes, transactions
- Revise OOP: all 4 pillars with code examples in Java or Python

**Days 21-40: Depth**
- 50 medium problems on linked lists, trees, graphs
- Build or polish ONE strong project — ideally full-stack
- Write detailed project documentation (you'll thank yourself in the interview)

**Days 41-55: Mock Rounds**
- Do 10+ mock interviews (friends, seniors, or AI-based tools)
- Practice explaining your project out loud until it's smooth
- Study 20 common HR questions with STAR-method answers

**Days 56-60: Polish**
- Review your resume — does it survive ATS? [Check your ATS score on CareerLens](/resume) to be sure
- Do 1 full mock loop (aptitude + tech + HR back-to-back)
- Sleep well. Don't study the night before.

## FAQ

**How long does the entire Infosys interview process take in 2026?**

From the online test to offer letter, expect **4-8 weeks** for freshers and **2-4 weeks** for lateral candidates. The online test results come within 7-10 days. If shortlisted, technical and HR rounds are usually scheduled within 2-3 weeks. Offer letters take another 1-2 weeks after HR clearance. During peak hiring windows (September-November and February-April), the process moves faster. If you're waiting beyond 4 weeks post-interview without updates, follow up with your Infosys contact — silence beyond that usually means a soft rejection.

**Is the Infosys 2-year service agreement legally enforceable?**

Yes, the ₹1 lakh service bond is legally enforceable in India for Infosys, but with conditions. Courts have upheld reasonable bonds that compensate for actual training costs. Infosys invests roughly ₹2.5-3 lakh per fresher in Mysore training, so a ₹1 lakh recovery is considered proportionate. However, you can negotiate waivers in specific situations — medical emergencies, higher education abroad, or marriage-related relocation. The bond does NOT prevent you from leaving — it just means you pay the amount if you exit before 24 months. Many candidates do leave early and pay the bond; it's a career decision, not a legal trap.

**Can I negotiate my CTC at Infosys as a fresher?**

Very limited scope for freshers. The System Engineer CTC of ₹3.6 LPA is fixed across all campuses — no negotiation. For Digital Specialist Engineer (₹6.5 LPA), there's zero flexibility too, but you can push for a faster promotion review at 12 months instead of 18. For Power Programmer offers, you can sometimes negotiate joining bonuses of ₹50K-1L if you have competing offers from Wipro WILP or TCS Digital. For lateral hires with 3+ years experience, aggressive negotiation is both possible and expected — target 50-60% hike minimum and use competing offers as leverage.

**What's the difference between System Engineer and System Engineer Specialist roles at Infosys?**

System Engineer (SE) is the base fresher role at ₹3.6 LPA with generic allocation — you could end up in testing, support, or basic development. System Engineer Specialist (SES) is a mid-tier role at around ₹5 LPA offered to candidates with better coding performance in the online test, who get allocated to specific technology tracks (Java, Python, cloud, data). SES candidates typically skip some basic Mysore training modules and reach billable projects 2-3 months faster. If you perform exceptionally well in your technical interview and have strong projects, ask the HR about SES consideration — it's not advertised but exists.

**Should I prefer Infosys over TCS or Wipro as a fresher in 2026?**

Depends on your goal. Infosys has slightly better training (Mysore campus is world-class), moderately better exposure to GenAI projects via Topaz, and faster promotion cycles than TCS. However, TCS has better job security and lower attrition pressure. Wipro's WILP program pays better upfront but has longer bonds. For pure learning in your first 2 years, Infosys edges out. For stability, TCS wins. For salary alone, none of them are competitive versus product companies — if your profile is strong enough for Infosys DSE or Power Programmer, you probably have a shot at mid-tier product companies paying ₹12-18 LPA. - **Target the right track** — System Engineer (₹3.6 LPA) is mass hiring; aim for Digital Specialist Engineer (₹6.5 LPA) or Power Programmer (₹11 LPA) if your coding is strong
